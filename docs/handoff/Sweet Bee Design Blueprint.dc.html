01

Creative concept

The Case

The organising idea is the pastry case itself. Every screen behaves the way the case behaves: a small number of extraordinary objects, arranged deliberately, described on tiny handwritten-feeling labels, rotating without warning. Ally's edge isn't that she bakes well — plenty of bakeries bake well — it's that you never know what's going to be in there. Scarcity, surprise and craft are the brand, so they become the interaction model, not just the photography.

Three references stack: a modern food publication (editorial pacing, oversized type, asymmetry, generous white), a neighbourhood bakehouse (warm cream paper, real handwriting, hours you have to plan around), and a working studio (visible process, three-day dough, unfinished ideas made public). The site reads as a magazine that happens to sell croissants, published by someone who is clearly in the kitchen.

The three ideas that carry it
The label. The little case tags become the site's one recurring UI object — off-white, hairline border, gold rule, rotated a degree or two.
The rotation. Seasonal-ness is designed in: a live “in the case today” line, seasonal marks, copy that admits things sell out.
The lamination. Layered compositions — images overlapping frames, type crossing images, panels sliding past each other.
The quality test

Could this site belong to another bakery with the logo swapped? No — because the structural ideas are Sweet Bee's own: a menu built as a case rather than a list, a headline about improvising after lamination, a press section called A little buzz, prices that admit a crab rangoon croissant exists.

“Everything looks better than you expected.”

Balance holds at roughly 75% cream, paper and photography / 15% teal family / 8% gold, with ink for type. Teal appears as whole panels rather than tints everywhere; gold never fills a large area except as a CTA sitting on deep teal, where it is the highest-contrast thing on screen. Ink on cream is 15.6:1; cream on deep teal is 8.7:1; ink on gold is 12.4:1 — every combination clears AA at body size and AAA at display size.

Typography

Editorial display
Newsreader
200–500, roman + italic
We laminate. Then we improvise.
Utility sans
Archivo
400 / 500 / 600
Small uppercase eyebrow — .18em tracking
Body copy, menu descriptions, prices, hours, navigation. Set 16–18px on the web at 1.65–1.72 line-height, never below 14px.
Brand script
Existing wordmark
Image asset only
Header, footer, story sign-off, packaging moments. Three appearances per page maximum. Never set as live text.

Display sizes step 90 / 78 / 62 / 44px on desktop and compress to 50 / 44 / 38px on mobile — the ratio between display and body widens on large screens rather than everything scaling together. Headlines break by hand (<br> plus text-wrap: balance) because line breaks are part of the composition.

Buttons

Explore the Menu →
Order Online ↗
Get Directions →

Three treatments only: filled teal (primary), gold-underlined type with an arrow (secondary), gold fill (primary on teal panels). 2px radius, never pills. Minimum 48px tall on touch.

The label object

Seasonal
Pistachio Cream
$7

Paper card, hairline border, gold rule, soft shadow, 0.6–1.4° rotation, always overlapping the image it names. This is the signature component and the only place rotation is allowed.

Spacing & radius

8px base; sections breathe at 104–152px vertical on desktop, 56–72px on mobile. 48px page gutter, 1440px measure. Radius is 2px everywhere — images, buttons, panels. Nothing is a rounded card.

Photography

No filters, no grain, no dark overlays on food. Type sits beside or below photography, not on top of it — the one exception is the mobile hero, where a bottom-anchored gradient covers the video's lower third only. Ratios deliberately vary: 3:4, 1:1, 4:5, full-bleed.

Icon & motion

The bee is a signature, not decoration: favicon, one hero eyebrow, one section marker, footer. Arrows and hairlines do the rest. Motion is 240–560ms, cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1), opacity and small translation only, and fully removable under reduced-motion.

Homepage blueprint

Nine movements, paced deliberately: two loud, one quiet, one loud, and so on. Full-bleed teal appears exactly twice on the page — the statement and the visit panel — so both land.

01

Hero — asymmetric split

Purpose
Establish craft and personality in one viewport, without utility clutter.
Layout
Type column left (1fr), vertical video right (470×626) with a gold hairline frame offset 16px behind it. A case label breaks the video's left edge.
Imagery
Bakery video, muted, looping, no controls. Photo fallback: chocolate croissants.
Type
90px Newsreader, second line italic in deep teal. Bee glyph + “A bakehouse in Pearland, Texas” eyebrow above.
Copy
Your neighborhood croissant maker. Plain-spoken and local; the craft shows in the photography, not the claim.
Interaction
Type rises 18px on load; video cross-fades in over 1.2s. CTAs: filled teal + gold-underlined. Hours and address sit as a quiet metadata line at the bottom.
Mobile
Video becomes a 430px full-bleed block with the headline over its lower third; CTAs stack full-width below.
02

“In the case today” band

Purpose
Prove the site is alive and the case rotates. A single teal rule of today's five items.
Behaviour
Static on desktop, centred; horizontally scrollable on mobile. Editable weekly — the one piece of content Ally is asked to touch. Not a marquee.
03

What's in the case?

Purpose
Product discovery without product cards.
Layout
Horizontal drag rail, six items, every image a different size and ratio (392×522, 308×412, 344×344…), baseline-aligned to the bottom so the tops stagger like a real case. Each image gets a hairline rule and a rotated label.
Type
78px “What's in the case?” left; short prose plus “See the full menu →” right, baseline-aligned.
Interaction
Drag / wheel / trackpad scroll, 3px hairline scrollbar. Hover lifts the label 2px and deepens its shadow. Rail intentionally overflows the gutter so it reads as continuing.
Mobile
Same rail, smaller crops, snap points, edge peek of the next item.
04

Brand statement — deep teal

Purpose
The one page-wide colour moment; the reset between product energy and story.
Layout
118px type, left-aligned, with the punchline pushed to the right edge in gold italic — the line break is the joke's timing.
Copy
We could have stopped at butter. We didn't. Below, small: “Somewhere between the traditional and the frankly unhinged is a croissant filled with crab rangoon. That's where we live.”
Interaction
Two-stage reveal on scroll: first line, then “We didn't.” 160ms later. Nothing else moves.
Mobile
50px, three lines, punchline right-aligned. Panel keeps its full-bleed weight.
05

The seasonal board — editorial spread

Purpose
Show range and craft; make lamination feel tactile.
Layout
One 760px-tall pistachio image at 1.15fr with a 266×332 crab rangoon crop breaking out of its bottom-right corner. Right column: eyebrow, 62px headline, two paragraphs, a two-stat rule (“3 days” / “Thu–Sat”), then a small captioned fig & ricotta image — deliberately below the fold of the section.
Interaction
Slow parallax: the small crop drifts ~24px against the large image. No hover state; this section is for reading.
Mobile
Recomposes rather than stacks: full-bleed crab rangoon image, then text, then the fig crop inline. The overlap is dropped rather than shrunk.
06

Hi, I'm Ally

Purpose
The credibility that only a person can give. Paper-white background so it reads as a different chapter.
Imagery
The supplied portrait of Ally plating peach desserts — 520×650, offset teal hairline frame behind it. Real, in-kitchen, unposed enough to be believable.
Copy
First person, three short paragraphs: edible art, the cottage-bakery origin in East Pearland, and “If you see something odd in the case, that's on purpose. Ask me about it.” Signed off with the script wordmark.
Mobile
Full-bleed portrait, headline and copy beneath; frame offset removed.

Editorial index, not a logo wall: hairline-ruled rows of publication / one-line context / “Press” tag. No borrowed logos, no star ratings.

04

Menu page blueprint

The signature experience: a digital pastry case. A scannable list on the left, a large photographic panel on the right that changes as you move down the list, and the case label as the connective object between them. Twenty-six items stay browsable in one screenful of scrolling while any single item can become a full photograph on demand.

Hero

112px “Everything in the case.” left; the overhead case photograph (440×540) right, captioned Thursday, 10:04 a.m. — a timestamp rather than a caption, reinforcing that this is a snapshot of a rotating case. Supporting line: “The classics, the seasonal experiments, and whatever Ally couldn't stop thinking about this week.”

Current favorites

Three oversized items before the list, at three different heights (520 / 404 / 600px), bottom-aligned, each labelled. Curated, credited as “three that keep selling out” — editorial recommendation, not a bestseller widget. Rotates with the season.

Category navigation

Structure
All · Originals · Seasonal · Sweets. Sticky below the header at 78px, on a translucent paper bar with a hairline base.
States
Active is a filled teal chip; inactive is a 5%-ink chip. Colour is not the only signal — the live item count beside the bar updates too. Keyboard-focusable with a 2px gold focus ring.
Behaviour
Filters in place with a 300ms fade; the photo panel resets to the first item of the new category. Group headings (“Croissants — Seasonal”) remain in All, so filtering is a convenience rather than the only way to navigate.
Mobile
Horizontally scrollable chip row, swipeable, sticky under a 250px photo strip.

Item row & photo panel

Row
27px Newsreader name, optional Seasonal outline tag, dotted leader, 20px price, 14px description below. Compact by design — the leader dots are the one menu cliché worth keeping because they make prices scannable.
Desktop
Hover or focus indents the row 16px, tints it 5% teal, and cross-fades the sticky 500×640 panel to that item's photograph over 560ms using two stacked layers, so images never flash.
No photo
Items without photography (about half) show a paper-toned panel with the item name, the bee mark, and “Photograph coming — it looks considerably better in person.” The gap becomes charm instead of a broken state.
Mobile
The panel becomes a sticky photo strip above the list; tapping a row updates it and expands the description. Every fact — name, price, description, seasonal status — is present without any hover.

Availability & order CTA

A teal-tinted note closes the list: “The case changes often. Seasonal flavors rotate when Ally gets an idea, and Saturday afternoons are unpredictable. Half the fun is not knowing what's on the tray.” Set at reading size, not fine print.

Then a deep-teal band: “Want it waiting for you?” with a gold Pre-order on Hotplate button. Hotplate is always an outbound link with an external-link mark — no fake cart, no invented checkout.

05

Secondary pages

Our Story

Lives on the homepage as an anchored chapter rather than a thin standalone page, and is reachable from the nav as Our Story. If Ally later has more to tell — process, the move from cottage to storefront, the people — it graduates to its own page built from three blocks: the portrait chapter, a three-day lamination sequence with process photography, and a “what's next” note. Recommended additional shoot: hands laminating dough, the oven, the 6 a.m. tray.

Visit

Also anchored on the homepage, and the destination for the nav's Visit. Location first: address, the three open days, directions. Add on the standalone version a static map image linking to Google Maps, parking and suite-A wayfinding (“the door is around the side”-type help if true), Instagram, and a single email link for inquiries. No contact form — the honest funnels are Hotplate, Instagram DMs and the cake inquiry. Email and phone still need confirming.

06

Navigation

Structure
Wordmark left; Home · Menu · Our Story · Visit centre-right in 11px uppercase Archivo at .15em; a hairline divider; then Order Online as the only filled element in the bar. Order Online is a different species of object, not a heavier link.
Scroll
78px tall, translucent cream with a 14px backdrop blur, gaining its hairline base once the page moves. It never shrinks, hides, or re-animates on scroll direction.
States
Hover and current page both draw a 1.5px gold underline; current page also sets ink at full strength. Keyboard focus is a 2px gold ring with 2px offset. Touch targets 44px minimum.
Mobile
Wordmark plus a two-line mark. Opening cross-fades in a full-height cream sheet: four 32px Newsreader links stacked, a gold rule, then Order Online as a full-width teal button and Instagram beneath. Sheet fades and the list rises 12px over 260ms — no slide-in drawer, no hamburger-to-X spin.
07

Footer

Concept
The last brand moment. Deep teal, cream type, and the script wordmark at 52px — the largest the script appears anywhere on the site — paired across the top rule with a gold italic line: “Get here before they're gone.”
Contents
Four columns: Find us (address + directions), Hours (closed days shown dimmed), Explore (Menu, Our Story, Visit), Follow & order (Instagram, Hotplate). Baseline: bee mark + “Sweet Bee Bakehouse — Pearland, Texas”.
Details
Gold links on hover only; no newsletter box, no social icon row, no “website by”. Mobile stacks to two columns then one, keeping address and hours first.
08

Motion storyboard

Six moments carry the whole experience. Everything else is static on purpose — the site is designed to be beautiful with motion switched off, and under prefers-reduced-motion each of these degrades to an instant state change with no loss of information.

01
Hero settle. Headline rises 18px into place while the video cross-fades up from black. One beat, then stillness.
900–1200ms, once
02
Menu photo cross-fade. The most important interaction on the site: two stacked layers swap opacity as you move down the list, so the panel dissolves between pastries instead of blinking.
560ms, per item
03
Row indent. The hovered menu row slides 16px right and takes a 5% teal wash — the digital equivalent of pulling one label forward in the case.
300ms
04
Statement punchline. “We could have stopped at butter.” clip-reveals upward, then “We didn't.” arrives 160ms later. Comic timing, done with a delay.
2 × 520ms
05
Case drag. Momentum-scrolled rail with labels lifting 2px as they pass under the cursor. The only horizontal movement on the homepage.
Input-driven
06
Bee transition. Between page loads the bee mark fades in centred on cream for ~400ms. The one place the icon animates, and the only branded transition.
400ms max

Explicitly excluded: scroll hijacking, parallax on more than one section, counters, marquees, spinning bees, custom cursors, and any entrance animation on body copy.

Responsive strategy

Editorial layouts recompose; they don't shrink. Three rules govern every breakpoint: overlaps are dropped rather than scaled, side-by-side panels become sequential full-bleed blocks, and display type drops a step rather than reflowing to five lines.

Large desktop
1440–1920
Content capped at 1440 and centred; full-bleed panels and rails still run edge to edge, so the extra width becomes cream margin rather than stretched layout. Display type at full scale.
Laptop
1024–1440
The reference design. Gutters 48→36px, hero headline 90→72px, hero video 470→400px wide, menu photo panel 500→420px. Overlaps and offsets survive intact.
Tablet
768–1024
Hero goes vertical: type block above, video full-width beneath at 16:9. The menu's photo panel moves from a sticky column to a sticky band above the list — the mobile pattern arrives early because two columns stop working before 1024. Editorial spread loses its breakout crop and becomes image / text / image.
Mobile
375–430
Designed, not derived — most visitors arrive from Instagram. Full-bleed vertical video with the headline over its lower third; stacked full-width CTAs; the case rail becomes a snap-scrolling swipe; the statement panel keeps full-bleed teal at 50px; the menu becomes photo strip + chips + compact list; the visit panel stacks photo over teal. Body text never below 14.5px, tap targets never below 44px.

Accessibility travels with it: every hover behaviour has a focus equivalent, no information exists only in a hover or a colour, focus rings are gold on cream and gold on teal, closed days are stated rather than omitted, and the reduced-motion build loses nothing but the transitions.

12

Distinctive design moments

01
The case label as the site's UI. Sweet Bee's physical price tags become the one recurring component — paper, gold rule, one degree off-square, always overlapping the photograph it names. It appears on the hero, the rail, the favorites and the menu panel, and it exists nowhere else on the internet.
02
A menu that behaves like a case, not a list. Twenty-six scannable rows on the left, one enormous pastry on the right, cross-fading as you move. Fast for the person who knows what they want, cinematic for the person browsing.
03
“In the case today.” A single teal rule under the hero listing what's actually on the tray this week — one editable line that makes the site feel like it was updated this morning, because it was.
04
Missing photos as a joke instead of a gap. “Photograph coming — it looks considerably better in person.” The half-finished nature of a real small bakery becomes part of the voice, and the menu can launch before the shoot.
05
One punchline, page-wide, in deep teal. “We could have stopped at butter. We didn't.” — 118px, two-stage reveal, the only place colour takes the whole screen. Restraint everywhere else is what makes it hit.
06
Ratios that refuse to match. No two images on the homepage share a size, and the rail is bottom-aligned so the tops stagger. It reads as a composed spread rather than a CMS grid — the single clearest signal that a person art-directed this.
